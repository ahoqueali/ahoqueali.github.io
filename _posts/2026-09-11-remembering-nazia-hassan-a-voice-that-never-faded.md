Some voices belong to a particular time. Others somehow remain with us long after the music has stopped.

**Nazia Hassan was one of those voices.**

<img src="/assets/images/nazia-hassan.png"
     alt="Nazia Hassan"
     loading="lazy">


Born in Karachi in 1965, Nazia became one of the most recognisable voices of South Asian pop music. Her breakthrough came as a teenager with *Aap Jaisa Koi*, followed by the hugely influential *Disco Deewane* and a series of songs that helped define the sound of an era.

What made Nazia special was not simply her success. There was a warmth, innocence and effortless quality in her voice that made her music feel timeless.

### A voice from another era

Listening to Nazia today can feel like opening a window into the 1980s — a time when music was changing rapidly and South Asian pop was finding a new identity.

Her songs with her brother Zoheb Hassan became part of the soundtrack of millions of lives. Her influence reached well beyond Pakistan, connecting audiences across India, Britain and the wider South Asian diaspora.

### Gone, but never forgotten

Nazia passed away in London on **13 August 2000**, aged just 35. Her life was far too short, but her music outlived her by decades.

There is something moving about hearing her voice today. The technology has changed, musical fashions have changed, and generations have grown up — yet the emotion in those recordings remains.

Perhaps that is the real measure of an artist.

**Nazia Hassan may have left us, but her voice never really did.**

*In memory of Nazia Hassan (1965–2000). May Allah grant her the highest place in Jannatul Firdaus. Ameen.*
