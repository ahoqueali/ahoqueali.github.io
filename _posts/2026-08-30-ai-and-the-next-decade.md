The next ten years could be one of the most consequential periods in modern history.

But the biggest change may not be technological.

It may be **psychological**.

For most of human history, intelligence, expertise and information have been scarce. Artificial intelligence is beginning to change that equation.

The important question is therefore not simply:

> **Will AI replace humans?**

It is:

> **What happens when intelligence becomes abundant and inexpensive?**

## 2026–2028: AI Becomes an Agent

Today's AI primarily answers questions.

The next generation will increasingly **perform tasks**.

Instead of:

> "Write this program."

The instruction becomes:

> "Build it, test it, deploy it and monitor it."

AI agents will increasingly operate computers, analyse data, write software and coordinate workflows.

AI moves from being a tool to becoming an **economic actor**.

---

## 2028–2032: Work Gets Reorganised

The disruption to employment may not arrive as sudden mass unemployment.

Instead, organisations will discover that a small number of highly capable people equipped with AI can accomplish work previously requiring much larger teams.

This will particularly affect:

- Software development
- Administration
- Customer service
- Marketing
- Research
- Accounting
- Data analysis
- Legal and professional services

The result will be smaller organisations with greater productive capacity.

The value of the worker will increasingly shift from **performing tasks** to **directing intelligent systems**.

---

## Intelligence Becomes a Commodity

When everyone can access powerful AI, intelligence itself becomes less of a competitive advantage.

Judgement becomes more important.

If an AI can analyse a problem for everyone, the valuable skill becomes knowing:

> **Which problem should we solve?**

This is similar to what happened when calculators made arithmetic cheap.

Arithmetic did not disappear.

Its economic value simply moved upward.

---

## The Internet Changes Again

The first internet connected:

**People → Websites → Information**

The emerging internet may increasingly become:

**AI Agents → Information → Action**

Instead of visiting ten websites to research a product, book a hotel or find a service, an AI agent could eventually perform the entire process.

This could fundamentally change the economics of the web.

Search traffic may become less important.

**AI agents may become the new gateway to information.**

---

## Trust Becomes Scarce

AI will make it increasingly easy to generate convincing:

- Text
- Images
- Videos
- Reviews
- Voices
- Advertisements

The paradox is that the more synthetic information we produce, the more valuable **authenticity and trust** become.

Verified identity, provenance, reputation and reliable data could become major economic assets.

In an AI-generated world:

> **Trust may become more valuable than information.**

---

## Robotics Brings the Second Wave

AI is currently transforming cognitive work.

The next major stage will combine AI with robotics.

The combination of:

**AI + Vision + Robotics + Cheap Hardware**

could increasingly automate physical tasks in:

- Manufacturing
- Warehousing
- Agriculture
- Logistics
- Construction
- Hospitality

The first wave automates **thinking**.

The second wave increasingly automates **doing**.

---

## The Institutional Problem

Technology moves quickly.

Institutions move slowly.

AI productivity could rise rapidly while education systems, employment laws, taxation and social security remain designed for an earlier economic model.

That gap could become one of the defining political challenges of the 2030s.

Expect growing debates about:

- AI taxation
- Universal basic income
- Employment
- Wealth distribution
- AI regulation
- Ownership of AI-generated value

---

# 2036: A Different Kind of Scarcity

If AI makes intelligence abundant, humans may increasingly value things that technology cannot easily commoditise:

**Trust.**

**Relationships.**

**Community.**

**Creativity.**

**Experience.**

**Purpose.**

This may be the greatest irony of the AI revolution.

As machines become increasingly intelligent, being human may become **more valuable precisely because it is scarce**.

The next decade therefore isn't simply a story about machines becoming smarter.

It is a story about **what humans decide is worth doing when intelligence is no longer scarce.**

---

*This is a forward-looking analysis, not a prediction of specific events. Historical patterns can identify plausible trajectories, but the future remains uncertain.*
