The previous post explored *what* might happen. This post is about *what to do about it*.

If intelligence becomes abundant and inexpensive, the competitive advantage shifts. Here is how to position yourself.

---

## 1. Move Up the Abstraction Ladder

**Stop optimising for execution. Start optimising for direction.**

AI handles execution increasingly well. The scarce skill is deciding *what* to execute and *why*.

- **Develop taste.** Learn to recognise quality, coherence, and strategic fit. Taste cannot be easily automated.
- **Practise problem framing.** The answer matters less than the question. Spend more time defining problems precisely.
- **Build judgement.** Judgement is the ability to make good decisions with incomplete information. It comes from experience, reflection, and feedback loops.

---

## 2. Become an AI Orchestrator, Not a Competitor

**Don't race the machine. Direct it.**

The most valuable workers of the 2030s will be those who can:
- Decompose complex goals into AI-executable tasks
- Evaluate and integrate AI outputs
- Know when *not* to use AI
- Design workflows where humans and AI each do what they do best

**Action:** Pick one workflow you own. Redesign it with AI as a collaborator. Document what works. Repeat.

---

## 3. Invest in Scarce Human Capitals

Some things become *more* valuable when intelligence is cheap:

| Capital | Why It Scarcifies | How to Build It |
|---------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Trust** | Synthetic content floods the zone | Consistent delivery, transparent communication, verified track record |
| **Relationships** | AI cannot build genuine rapport | Invest in people deeply; help without immediate ROI |
| **Taste/Judgement** | Infinite options require curation | Study excellent work; articulate *why* it works |
| **Physical Presence** | Digital becomes saturated | Show up in person; build local reputation |
| **Accountability** | AI has no skin in the game | Put your name on outcomes; own failures |

---

## 4. Build a Portfolio of Bets, Not a Single Career Path

The half-life of specialised skills is shrinking. A single linear career is fragile.

Instead, build a **portfolio**:
- **Core competency:** One deep skill you're known for (compound this)
- **Adjacent skills:** 2–3 complementary capabilities that expand your optionality
- **Exploration bets:** 10–20% of time learning something entirely new
- **Income streams:** Aim for 2+ sources (employment + consulting + products + investments)

---

## 5. Master the Meta-Skill: Learning How to Learn

Specific knowledge expires. The ability to acquire new knowledge rapidly does not.

- **Build a personal knowledge system.** Capture, connect, retrieve. Tools matter less than the habit.
- **Practice rapid prototyping.** Idea → test → learn → iterate in days, not months.
- **Seek tight feedback loops.** Environments where reality corrects you quickly (markets, code, craft, sport).
- **Learn in public.** Teaching forces clarity; visibility creates opportunity.

---

## 6. Secure Your Financial Foundation

Economic transitions create volatility. Financial resilience buys you agency.

- **Eliminate high-interest debt.** It constrains your ability to take risks.
- **Build 12–24 months of runway.** Cash reserves let you say "no" to bad fits and "yes" to asymmetric bets.
- **Diversify income geography.** Remote work + multiple currencies = geographic arbitrage.
- **Understand AI's impact on your industry's economics.** Position before the shift hits your P&L.

---

## 7. Cultivate Physical and Mental Durability

The next decade will be cognitively demanding. Your biology is your infrastructure.

- **Sleep, movement, nutrition.** Non-negotiable. They compound.
- **Attention control.** The ability to do deep work in a distraction-saturated world is a superpower.
- **Stress inoculation.** Voluntary discomfort (cold, fasting, heavy lifting, difficult conversations) raises your threshold.
- **Community.** Isolation is a risk factor. Invest in people who challenge and support you.

---

## 8. Develop a Personal AI Strategy

Treat AI like a strategic resource, not a toy.

1. **Audit your workflows.** Where do you spend time on low-judgement, high-volume tasks?
2. **Experiment systematically.** One new tool/workflow per week. Measure time/quality delta.
3. **Build custom tools.** Fine-tune, RAG, or prompt-engineer for your specific context. Generic AI = generic output.
4. **Stay current, not current-events.** Follow 3–5 deep technical sources, not daily news.

---

## 9. Think in Decades, Act in Weeks

Long-term orientation with short-cycle execution.

- **Decade vision:** Where do you want to be in 2036? What capabilities, relationships, assets, reputation?
- **Annual themes:** One focus area per year (e.g., "Year of Building," "Year of Distribution," "Year of Depth")
- **Quarterly OKRs:** 3–5 measurable objectives
- **Weekly reviews:** What worked? What didn't? What changes next week?

---

## 10. Remember: The Goal Is Not to Predict the Future

It is to **build a position that improves across multiple futures**.

A resilient position has:
- **Optionality** (multiple ways to win)
- **Anti-fragility** (gains from disorder)
- **Compounding assets** (skills, relationships, capital, reputation that grow without constant input)
- **Alignment** (work that energises rather than drains you)

---

## A Concrete Starting Point (This Week)

1. **Map your current workflows.** Identify the top 3 where AI could 10x output.
2. **Pick one relationship** you've neglected. Reconnect with specificity (not "let's catch up").
3. **Set up a learning system** if you don't have one. Obsidian, Notion, Logseq—pick one, start today.
4. **Check your runway.** Calculate months of expenses covered. Set a target.
5. **Write your 2036 vision.** One page. Private. Specific.

---

The next decade belongs to those who treat change as a design material, not a threat.

Start building.
